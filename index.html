One donation can reach more than one person

Why regular donors matter

Whole blood is separated into red cells, platelets and plasma, so a single donation can help up to three patients.

Blood doesn’t keep for long. Red cells last about six weeks and platelets only about a week, so hospitals need steady donors rather than one-off drives.

Most healthy adults can give whole blood every three months. LifeDrop marks you as ready again 90 days after your last donation.

“Donating makes you weak.”

Most healthy adults feel fine after resting and having a drink and a snack. The body replaces the fluid within a day and the red cells within a few weeks.

“Needles can spread infection.”

Donation kits are sterile and used only once.

“My blood group isn’t needed.”

Every group is needed. Rarer groups such as O negative and AB negative are often the hardest to find in an emergency.

What happens when you donate

  1. Register and answer a few questionsA short health questionnaire and photo ID.
  2. Quick health checkPulse, blood pressure and haemoglobin.
  3. DonateThe donation takes about 10 minutes.
  4. Rest and refreshHave a drink and a snack, and take it easy for the rest of the day.
